# Production locks

> These are not opinions. Every item comes from a 199-ad classification of one DTC account in the men's health / ED supplement vertical (May to September 2026), and **each one survived both the cut that excludes May and the cut restricted to a single product**.
>
> A violation fails the quality gate. If a lock conflicts with the writer's taste, the lock wins.

---

## LOCK 1 — three proof modalities in every copy
**0.427 against 0.192 for copies that use two. More than 100% difference.** It is the most robust finding in the batch and the only one that persists across every cut tested.

Pick exactly **three** from:
- `personal_experience` — the narrator lived it
- `mechanism_logic` — the explanation that makes the result inevitable
- `verifiable_number` — a lab value, a study, a timeframe, a dose
- `third_party_validation` — someone else noticed
- `demonstration` — the scene that proves without asserting

Two is the floor that fails. Four was not tested and is not a virtue.

## LOCK 2 — name the product
**0.411 against 0.177.** Withholding the name to create curiosity cuts ROAS by more than half. The brand name appears in the copy, spelled out.

## LOCK 3 — product explicit and late
**0.416 (late) against 0.322 (early).** The product enters after the mechanism has already absolved the reader, never in the opening.

And no **trick, secret or hack**: `trick_hack` is the worst group in the entire batch, **0.195**, with a cost per checkout almost double the average.

## LOCK 4 — a turn narrative, not a direct sell
**0.417 against 0.345.** The copy is a story that turns, not an argument that stacks benefits.

## LOCK 5 — the villain is biology
**0.447 against 0.317 for a doctor or system villain.** The hormone is the culprit. The doctor shows up as a **failure** (the normal chart), not as the villain. The industry and the pill go in the enemy block, never as the main culprit.

## LOCK 6 — dollar price in the copy
**0.543 with a dollar price, 0.355 with no price, 0.246 with a per-day price.** The per-day framing is the worst of the three, which contradicts the instinct to dilute the price.

## LOCK 7 — explicit register, intermediate aggression
Explicit language wins in both languages the account ran: **0.536 and 0.414, against 0.259 and 0.365 for the sober register**. Because it wins in both, the effect is register, not language.

But the **aggressive package loses**: conspiracy, manufactured scarcity and an unnamed product. Intermediate aggression does 0.467 against 0.297 for conservative and 0.333 for aggressive.

Operational translation: **you can go up on the spicy, you cannot go up on the conspiratorial.**

## LOCK 8 — direct-purchase CTA
**0.412 against 0.191 for learn more.** When the round's destination is a quiz, the CTA drives straight to the quiz with a direct verb, never a lukewarm learn more.

## LOCK 9 — third-party validation
The result is **never** declared by the user himself. Someone else notices it: the wife, the trainer, the friends, the doctor at the next appointment. It shows up in practically every winning copy in the account.

Failed: "I feel incredible", "I have never felt better", "it changed my life".
Passed: she asked me what I changed; my trainer pulled me aside; my ex texted me.

## LOCK 10 — the lab value always carries a number
Between **350 and 412**, to hold up the contradiction between a normal chart and real life. A specific number is implicit proof; "low testosterone" is not.

## LOCK 11 — no em dashes and no quotation marks
Fixed house rule for ad copy output, applies across every skill. Dialogue is marked with a line break, never with quotation marks. A pause is marked with a period or a comma, never with an em dash.

## LOCK 12 — the first three lines carry the see more
On Meta, long copy is truncated. The first three lines decide whether the rest gets read. They have to work as a closed block that raises the question without handing over the answer.

## Hypothesis to test (not a lock)

**A 30-day guarantee beats a 90-day one: 0.486 against 0.296**, inside the same product. Policy stays at 90 days. The suspicion is that "90 days" makes the reader hear "it takes 90 days to work". Worth one cell with the framing changed, not a policy change.

---

## What the entire batch never used (the opportunity)

Across 199 ads, these never appeared:
- **personal authority proof** (a narrator with his own credential) — the gap the authority angle tests for the first time
- **investigative editorial format**
- **listicle format**
- **personal letter and urgent-news format**

Absence is information. Those four are the cheapest gap in the account.
