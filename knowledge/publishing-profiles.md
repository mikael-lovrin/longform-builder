# Publishing profiles — the pages that run the ads

> Pattern measured in a public Ad Library sweep of a competitor in the same vertical (88 active ads, 70 distinct creatives, collected 2026-09-23): the same offer runs simultaneously through **13 profiles**, and the brand page accounts for **4 of the 88 ads**. The bulk comes out of person pages and pseudo-authority pages.

## What the competitor does, measured

| Page type | How many, and their active ad counts | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Person, male | 4 pages: 70, 49, 32, 29 active ads | the biggest volume in the operation |
| Person, female | 1 page: 7 | smaller, but present |
| Couple | 1 dedicated couple page: 43 | built for the couple angle |
| Pseudo-authority | 3 pages: 108, 102, 24 | where the urologist long forms live |
| Community / niche | 2 pages: 63, 16 | |
| Brand | 206 ads in total, only 4 in this sweep | barely runs long form |

Two numbers that orient everything: **one pseudo-authority page alone carries 108 active ads**, and the dominant destination is a quiz funnel (57 of the 88). The button is `Learn more` in 66 of the 88.

The read: they do not have one page with many ads, they have **many pages with many ads**, each one matched to a type of narrator.

## Why this exists

First-person long form static **does not work coming off the brand page**. A supplement brand does not have a husband, does not have a blood panel and does not wake up at 1:47 in the morning. The voice of the copy needs a compatible face, or the whole piece reads false before the third paragraph.

On top of that, spreading across pages isolates risk: one restriction does not take down the whole round.

## Every batch names its page

Each draft declares the page in its frontmatter (`profile:`), spelled **exactly as the page is named on Meta** (punctuation included). The name comes out in the main document (PROFILE field) and in the `profile` column of `upload.csv`, so whoever uploads the ad never has to guess which page runs it. Keep a list of the pages that actually exist in the round file, and do not assign a page that has not been created yet.

## The congruence rule

**The page has to match the angle's POV.** Running a dead-bedroom copy narrated by a wife off a men's health authority page breaks congruence and kills the read.

| Angle | Copy POV | Page type |
|---|---|---|
| Angle 1 Partner running a protocol | female | person, woman |
| Angle 2 Dead bedroom, present tense | female | person, woman |
| Angle 3 Male confession | male | person, man |
| Angle 4 Authority | authority | men's health / education |
| Angle 5 Single villain | neutral / explanatory | men's health / education |

## Recommended profile archetypes

Pick a name for each persona and keep it consistent across the round. Names go in the copy unmarked, per the placeholder policy in the round file.

### Person pages, female voice (Angles 1 and 2)

**1. The confessional wife.**
Woman, 49, married 22 years, two teenagers, lives in the suburbs. Works part time in school administration. Communication: confessional, writes like someone venting in a group chat and then regretting it a little. Uses specific domestic detail (the time on the stove clock, the brand of coffee). Never speaks as an expert: she learned it from another woman. She is the Silent Protocol persona.

**2. The blunt nurse.**
Woman, 46, married 18 years, shift nurse. Communication: more direct and rawer than the first one, a little acid humor, no euphemisms about sex. She is the one who can carry the most explicit column without sounding vulgar, because that is already her register. Good for Angle 2, where the pain is in the present.

### Person pages, male voice (Angle 3)

**3. The plain-spoken tradesman.**
Man, 54, divorced, works in construction or logistics. Communication: dry, short, admits the shame without dramatizing it. Sentences under ten words. He is the opposite of a coach: he is not teaching anything, he is telling you what happened. Good for the confession.

**4. The recent retiree.**
Man, 58, married, recently retired. Communication: reflective, slightly longer, the tone of someone who has already processed it. Serves as a register variation for the same angle, for when the first male voice saturates.

### Authority (Angle 4) — contracted physician

**5. The physician's own page.**
**The doctor is contracted and real**: the brand contracts the professional who signs and publishes the copy, so the angle runs as genuine personal-authority proof, in the first person and in a professional capacity. That is what unlocks Angle 4, which is the proof modality the account never used across 199 ads.

Communication: didactic and direct, no jargon. Explains plumbing, not biochemistry. Admits what conventional medicine does not solve without attacking colleagues. The credential appears in the first line and is never mentioned again.

**Name in the copies:** write the physician's name as the final name, unmarked. See the placeholder policy in the round file.

### Education and mechanism (Angle 5)

**6. The editorial mechanism page.**
Faceless editorial page. Communication: explanatory, mechanism first, plumbing language. Publishes about blood flow, cortisol and male aging. The natural home for Angle 5.

**7. The popular men's health page.**
Men's health page with a more popular register, closer to gym vocabulary. A second home for Angle 5 and for single-villain tests.

### Reserve

**8. A third female voice** — woman, 51, for when the first two saturate.
**9. The brand page** — remarketing and most aware only. It does not run first-person long form.

## What each page needs before it goes live

- Profile and cover photo consistent with the persona, no obvious stock imagery
- 8 to 15 organic posts predating the first ad, spaced across at least two weeks
- A short, compatible bio
- No mention of the brand in the organic content of person pages

A page with no organic history is the first one to fall in a review.

## The line you do not cross

A fictional persona is normal DR practice. **Three things change the nature of the problem** and must not be crossed:

1. **Using the name, photo or identity of a real person.** That includes a stock photo of an identifiable person presented as the person who wrote the copy.
2. **Fabricating the credential of a licensed professional.** The rule that stands: **medical authority copy only ships under a real professional who agreed to sign it.** If a future round proposes a second doctor, the path is to contract first and publish after, never to publish and resolve it later.
3. **The name or seal of a real news organization, government body or armed force.**

These three are not tone preferences. They are what separates a round you can scale from one that creates liability.
